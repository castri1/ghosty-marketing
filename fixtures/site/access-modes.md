---
title: Who can use your app
description: The four access modes — public, invite only, workspace members, and your own sign-in — and how to pick the right one.
order: 8
---

When you create an app you choose who can use it. That's the app's
**access mode**, and you can always see it on the app's Settings tab.
There are four:

## Public

Anyone with the link can open and use the app. Pick this for things you
want the world to see — a marketing site, a public tool, a demo.

## Invite only

The app is closed to everyone except people who sign up through your
**invite link**. You get the link when the app is ready and can share it
however you like; it also lives on the app's Settings tab.

If the link leaks, use **Rotate link** in Settings: the old link stops
working immediately, everyone is signed out, and you get a fresh link to
share with the right people.

## Workspace members

*Available on company workspaces.* The app is open to exactly the people
on your workspace team — no separate list to maintain, no link to share.

- **What your team sees.** Opening the app takes them to a sign-in page
  carrying your workspace branding. They sign in with their work email —
  either a one-time email link or **Sign in with Google** — and land back
  in the app. No passwords, nothing to install.
- **Managing who's in.** Membership is your workspace team, managed in
  one place: **Settings → Team**. Add someone there and they can use every
  members app right away; remove them and their access ends — someone with
  the app already open may keep it for up to 24 hours before they're asked
  to sign in again (and can't).
- **Need everyone out right now?** Use **Sign everyone out** on the app's
  Settings tab. Every current session ends immediately; members just sign
  back in. This is the fast lever when someone leaves and 24 hours is too
  long to wait.
- Members sign-in works everywhere the app answers — its Ghosty address,
  preview versions, and any custom domain you've connected.

## My own sign-in

The app handles its own accounts: you (or your AI assistant) build the
sign-up, sign-in, and permissions inside the app itself. Pick this when
your users aren't your teammates — customers, subscribers, a community —
and you want full control over the experience.

## Changing your mind

The access mode is chosen when the app is created and can't be switched
afterwards yet. If an app needs a different one — say a prototype went
public and should now be members only — create a fresh app with the right
mode and move the code over.
